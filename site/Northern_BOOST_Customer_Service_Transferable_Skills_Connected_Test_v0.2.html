Why a pizza restaurant? In one shift, the work can involve solving practical problems, organizing details, helping coworkers, responding to customers, improving processes, and making decisions under pressure. Those ways of working show up across healthcare, manufacturing, skilled trades, transportation, offices, technology, and many other workplaces.

Your O*NET interests give us a starting hypothesis. The scenarios help you notice two different signals: how you respond and whether you actually like working that way.

Rosie sets the stage:

This restaurant is our shared workplace simulation. The goal is to notice the strengths you use, how you approach different kinds of work, and which of those strengths you want opportunities to keep nurturing.

STEP 3 • EXPERIENCE THE WORK

Eight situations. Two kinds of evidence.

You will see eight restaurant situations: two connected to each of your top three O*NET interests, plus two intentionally different situations.

First: choose what you would do. That gives us evidence about your current workplace judgment.

Then: after you see the strongest response, Rosie will ask a reflection question about the specific work pattern you just used. Your preference—not whether you got the answer right—guides your development output.
Rosie says:

You can make a strong workplace decision and still decide you do not want more work like it. You can also struggle with a situation and realize you want to get better at it.
